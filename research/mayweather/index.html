The Money Floyd built.

A fight-by-fight accounting of Floyd Mayweather Jr.'s professional career, with the 2006 buyout from Top Rank marked as the inflection that turned a contracted fighter into a self-promoting empire.

50 fights · 1996 – 2017 Record: 50 – 0 Disclosed earnings: ~$807,954,000 Career total est: $1.1 B+

For the first decade of his career, Mayweather earned what a contracted fighter earns: a purse. His promoter, Top Rank, captured everything else — the gate, the pay-per-view upside, the ancillary revenue. In April 2006, Mayweather paid Bob Arum $750,000 to exercise a buyout clause in his contract. He founded Mayweather Promotions the following year. The financial structure of every subsequent fight changed: guaranteed purse, plus back-end percentage of PPV, plus international rights, plus gate, plus merchandise, plus the promoter's fee retained in-house. Methodology

Guaranteed Purse reflects the minimum guarantee filed with the Nevada State Athletic Commission prior to each bout, where disclosed. Pre-1998 bouts often went unreported by purse; "undisclosed" indicates a flat purse was paid but never made public.

Total Earnings reflects the reported all-in take per fight, including back-end PPV percentage, international broadcast rights, live gate share, sponsorship, and merchandise — components Mayweather only captured after founding Mayweather Promotions in 2007. Pre-2006 totals are marked with a dash because no back-end structure existed; Mayweather earned only the contracted fighter purse and Top Rank retained promotional revenue.

Disclosed figures may understate true earnings. Forbes and other outlets have estimated Mayweather's career total at $1.1–1.3 billion, accounting for revenue not captured in public filings.
